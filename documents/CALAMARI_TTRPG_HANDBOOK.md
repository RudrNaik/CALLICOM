# **`Character Creation`**

## **`Attributes`**

Attributes are your ‘core’ skills and are divided into 4 categories:

**Alertness-** encompasses hand-eye coordination, reflexes, attentiveness to detail, and overall awareness. 

**Body \-** represents a combination of the character’s body frame, general flexibility, and overall muscle tone. 

**Intelligence \-** is the character’s ability to use their mind in innovative ways and to learn and recall information. 

**Spirit \-** is a measure of the character’s charm and wit, but also of their determination and willpower.  

Each character starts with their attributes at zero. Zero is the number an average person has in all his abilities. The player has 5 points (or 150 XP) they can spend across the four categories, with each category having a cap of 4 points. Scores above 4+ are essentially superhuman or mechanical only and beyond the scope of most characters.

## **`Derived Attributes`**

| Combat Sense: 1 \+ Intelligence \+ Spirit  | Health: Average of Body and Spirit, rounded up. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **System Shock:** 5 \+ Health  | **Stamina:** 5 \+ Body \+ Spirit |
| **Unarmed Damage:** 3 \+ Body \+ CQC skill, rounded up | **Flesh Wound Threshold:** Stamina / 2, rounded up \+ Armor Class |
| **Armed Damage:** 3 \+ Body \+ Melee skill, rounded up | **Deep Wound Threshold:** Stamina \+ Armor Class |
|  | **Instant Death Threshold**: Stamina x 2   |

***Combat Sense*** is used primarily to detect ambushes and for initiative purposes. Initiative is rolled at the beginning of each combat round and rolling a fumble means that you cannot act that round. Characters that roll 7+ on their Combat Sense test may take one extra action without penalties. If for some reason a character's Combat Sense value is zero or below, treat the roll as an unskilled test (see the Dice Rolling and Tests tab for details).

***Health*** rates a character’s resistance to illness, toxins, and physiological shock. People who never seem to get sick and those who can drink large quantities of alcoholic beverages have a high Health rating. 

***Stamina*** is a rating of how much sheer physical punishment a character’s body can tolerate. While high Stamina will decrease the chances of injury, it is still quite easy for a character to be wounded by gunfire or other deadly weapons. Stamina is used to work out the Wound Thresholds, which determine when and if a character gets hurt. 

***Unarmed Damage*** is the Damage of any CQC skill attacks performed by the character.  

***Armed Damage*** is the Damage of any Melee skill attacks the character performs.  

***System Shock*** is a measure of how many injuries a character can take before going into shock and dying/becoming incapacitated. If the character’s total wound penalties surpass the system shock threshold, they go into Shock and are DBNO, or even Incapacitated.

# **`Skills`**

Skills are subcategories to attributes but their uses are not exclusively limited to them thanks to each character's chosen class, detailed further below. Each character gets 40 skill points total, the first 25 of which are defined by the chosen class of the character, and the other 15 are for the player to choose what to spend them on.

Class allotted skill points are divided into 25 dedicated to making four skills level 2, and five additional skills level 1\. These skills define the core aspects of the character's chosen class, for example, a rifleman’s core skills lay in their ability to use medium weapons like assault rifles and battle rifles as well as utilizing their throwing skills to toss grenades.

## **`Alertness Skills`**

**Light Weapons**  
**Specializations:** Specific weapon families  
Use pistols, revolvers, submachine guns and carbines. Includes basic knowledge of the maintenance procedures. 

**Medium Weapons**  
**Specializations:** Specific weapon families  
Use assault rifles, shotguns, DMRs, and sniper rifles. Includes basic knowledge of the maintenance procedures. 

**Heavy Weapons**  
**Specializations:** Specific Weapons families  
Use  Light Machine Guns, Rocket Launchers, Anti-Material Rifles, and Grenade Launchers. The skill also includes basic knowledge of the maintenance procedures. 

**Throw**  
**Specializations:** Grenades, Knives, improvised objects  
The Throw skill is the ability to accurately throw an object at a target. It is the skill used when throwing grenades and other offensive weapons. 

**Stealth**  
**Specialization:** sneaking, doing specific actions quietly  
Stealth is your measure of moving or doing actions quietly. To carry out a stealth action a character must roll their stealth against the enemy's alertness as an opposed roll. Success in this can net the character anything from carrying out their desired action successfully, moving without being noticed, or giving the character a heavy cover defense bonus until firing. 

**Tinker**  
**Specialization:** Vehicle repair, Drone repair, Gadget repair, creating improvised explosives, lockpicking.   
The Tinker skill allows a character to repair, modify, and or create machinery under time constraints (jury-rig a temporary repair), poor conditions (lack of proper tools),  or modify equipment in novel and desperate ways (modify a car engine into an IED). Items built or modified using this Skill, will only last as long as needed, and then fall apart; if used by anyone else, they also fall apart. A new Tinker test against the creation Threshold must be made whenever the item is pushed or takes damage; if failed, the item suffers a malfunction that requires one full-round action to fix. If fumbled, the item falls apart. 

**Drive**  
**Specialization:**  driving cars, driving tanks, flying helicopters, driving boats, flying drones    
Drive does exactly what it says, measuring your knowledge and skill driving land, sea and air vehicles, as well as combat drones. This can be measured in terms of checks to see if you can drive vast distances, or if you can drive under pressure. For example if you are driving while you are being chased, you may have to take a roll to escape your pursuers or whether you can avoid your vehicle being damaged by them. Failure driving can have dire consequences, ranging from vehicle damage making future drive checks harder, or in the worst cases can lead to you crashing.

## **`Body Skills`**

**Acrobatics**  
**Specialization:** Balancing, gymnastics, dodging  
The Acrobatics skill is the ability to perform activities requiring balancing, and gymnastics

**Athletics**  
**Specialization:** Running, parkour, physical fitness, swimming   
Athletics represents the character’s ability to engage in physical activities. It also includes the necessary skills, knowledge of the rules and regulations, and the physical training required by athletic activities.

**Endurance**  
**Specialization:** Long-distance running, rucking, swimming in rough conditions, hunkering down, pain threshold   
Endurance skill determines how conditioned the character is to endure various long-time exertions. Running chases sometimes may be solved with a contested Endurance test.

**Climb**  
**Specialization:** Free Climb, Assisted Climb, Rappelling  
This skill determines how well and fast a character can climb or rappel.

**Jump**  
**Specialization:** High Jump, Long Jump, rolled landing  
Use this skill to determine how far or high a character can jump. To jump, a character must make a Jump test, modified by Body and the defensive modifier of the character’s current movement. The basic Threshold is 3; this is modified by the condition(s) of the terrain (+3 if rough; \+6 if slippery; etc.). 

**Melee:**  
**Specialization:** Knives, Swords, Improvised weapons, Blunt weapons  
The Melee skill reflects how good a character is at attacking and/or defending with close-combat weapons, such as knives, swords, and bayonets. This type of weapon use is probably the most widespread of all since it involves relatively little training and mostly inexpensive weapons, yet increases the amount of damage dramatically. 

**CQC**  
**Specialization:** Grappling, Disarming, Tripping, Striking  
The CQC skill measures the proficiency of a character in close-range combat using unarmed fighting techniques. A high rating in CQC suggests that the character is using some form of martial art, such as karate or boxing.

## **`Mind Skills`**

**Politics**  
**Specialization:** Geopolitics, Corporate politics, National Politics  
Politics is the ability to manage and stay in the loop of the political affairs of a business, nation, or military. This can range from understanding the inner workings of a corporation or faction or maneuvering within a company or group. Some player characters are likely to take this skill test based on their background, whether they hold a senior position within a PMC or are trying to understand the rival motives of their enemy within the armed forces. In a more action-oriented sense, the skill is more limited.   

**Tech**  
**Specializations:** Coding, Hacking, Drone operation, Gadgets, Interfacing  
The Tech skill covers the use and maintenance of technically complicated items and gadgets. These range from drones that operators may bring into the field or hand-held gadgets that aid them in their preferred role. This skill also encompasses a character's know-how with computers, allowing them to hack enemy computer systems to gather intel or take control of drones and turrets. It is one of the many technical skills for mechanic-type characters to choose from since high-tech gadgets and computers are a common sight. 

**Forgery**  
**Specializations:** counterfeiting, writing, art, jewelry, mimickry   
The Forgery Skill is the character’s ability to accurately duplicate a variety of objects, such as official documents, works of art, money, and handwriting. It also encompasses the ability to recognize a forgery as such and give an opinion as to the origin of the forgery based on the style and materials used in creating it. If a character attempts to use his skill to work in a related but unfamiliar field, a \-2 penalty is imposed on the test. 

**Investigation**  
**Specializations:** Searching, surveillance, intelligence gathering, connecting dots  
The Investigation skill allows the character to collect information on people, places, and events. This information can be gathered by any means not covered by the Connections or Tech Skills. Player Characters may want to have this skill to one degree or another since it allows them to uncover facts about their antagonists or intelligence that might otherwise remain hidden.  

**Medicine**  
**Specializations:** First Aid, Combat Trauma, Nanomachines, Surgery  
Medicine is the ability to diagnose and treat various traumatic injuries and pathological infections or diseases. Medicine skill is also required for anything from advanced first aid, treating serious traumatic injuries, fighting off disease and infection, and intensive surgery.

**Navigate**  
**Specializations:** Night Time Navigation, Urban Navigation, Forest Navigation, Sense of Direction, geospatial intelligence  
Navigation represents the character’s proficiency in the various techniques used to track one’s positions and movements. 

**Trivia**  
**Specializations:** History, social, science, Law  
While nowhere as specialized as true scientific skills (which are beyond the scope of this game), successful use of the Trivia skill may allow the character to recall useful bits of information on various different topics.

## **`Spirit Skills`**

**Disguise**  
**Specializations:** Camouflage, Woodland concealment, Urban Concealment, Hidden in Shadows, Impersonation  
This skill covers everything regarding physically masking yourself from the enemy through a combination of outfits, camouflage, and adaptation to a character's environment. Actual impersonation of a specific individual may be more difficult. The skill pairs well with the stealth skill in enabling the character to better conceal themselves. Rolls are similar to stealth checks where a character must roll their Disguise against the enemy's alertness as an opposed roll. 

**Etiquette**  
**Specializations:** Chain of Command, Social Customs, Business, Specific Languages  
Etiquette is all about how you act and talk to different people across society to not offend others or talk out of line. If a character attempts to use his skill to interact in a related but unfamiliar milieu, a \-2 penalty is imposed on the test.

**Intimidate**  
**Specializations:** Stare Down, Threaten, Brutalisation, ‘Bad Cop’  
Intimidate is all about putting the fear of god into your opponent, whether it be to obtain information or to terrify enemies into a retreat or surrender.  

**Leadership**  
**Specializations:** Inspire, Rally, Lead from the Front  
Leadership is the capacity to lead others, either by example or through inspiration. With inspired and rallied characters, they can receive a bonus from the leadership until the leadership is killed or injured.  

**Persuasion**  
**Specializations:** Diplomacy, Negotiation, Bribery, ‘Good Cop’, Seduction  
Art of the deal and talking with a silver tongue, persuasion is a far more subtle means of obtaining information or persuading an enemy to surrender. It may also be used for more *personal* interactions between characters but this is left to the GM's discretion. 

**Connections:**   
**Specializations:** Affiliation to an Intelligence Agency, Connections to Criminal Elements, Friend in a High Place, Ear for Rumours  
Connections are all about obtaining information that may not be common knowledge, leaning on rumors and whispers from friends up high. 

**Notice**  
**Specializations:** Tracking, night sight, hiding spots, specific senses, minor details  
Notice is the ability to perceive details that may be otherwise overlooked. This includes finding a hidden entrance, identifying a key target, locating important intel, etc. A careful observer may use the skill to empathize with the person they are observing and can sometimes make prudent assumptions about the inner workings of the target.

# **`Character Progression`**

Every mission, or from specific achievements, tasks, and more- each character is rewarded with anywhere between one and five XP points, as the GM sees fit. XP points can be spent in a number of ways to allow your character to grow throughout a campaign.

## **`Skill Improvement`**

The main way XPs may be spent is through the purchase of new skill points. All new skill points must be purchased at level one and it is recommended that players develop a background element to how their characters acquired that skill. Skills are improved one at a time; levels cannot be skipped over in order to save XP.

| Skill Level | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| XP Cost | 1 | 5 | 15 | 30 |

## **`Skill Specialization`**

A character may obtain a Skill Specialization for 5 XP. This gives the character a \+1 bonus to his Skill test totals under certain conditions. For example, a rifleman may choose a specialization in the Assault Rifle family: Bullpup Rifles- thus adding a \+1 bonus to every roll made while using any form of Bullpup Rifle. The skill descriptions include suggested specializations, but the Gamemaster and players can add more should they wish. A character may purchase multiple different specializations in the same Skill, but no more than one specialization (i.e. \+1 bonus, maximum) can be applied on a single die roll. 

## **`Attribute Improvement`**  

Improving the character’s Attributes is possible but costly in XP to reflect the time and deep commitment required. Raising one Attribute by one point costs 30 XP. Alternatively, if your character completes an arc and with GM approval, you can also improve an Attribute by one. An attribute may usually not be raised higher than \+3 this way. Beyond the purely rule-oriented issues, the player should also come up with detailed background information on the process – perhaps his character spends his free nights at the gym or the library?  

## **`Emergency Dice`**  

Everyone gets a certain number of lucky breaks in life. To represent these lucky breaks, any or all XP earned can be converted to Emergency Dice, up to a max of four, which are then spent to boost crucial die rolls. Each XP spent buys one Emergency Die. When spent, these are added to the dice already available for the roll. Unless the Gamemaster disagrees, up to four Emergency Dice may be spent on any single roll.

Emergency Dice can also be spent to save a character’s life. When a player’s character is faced with something that would kill them, the player can opt to spend an Emergency Dice and escape death. This is slightly situational though. One cannot dodge a nuke. In combat, if an Emergency Dice is used to negate an instant death, the character instead takes two deep wounds. This can still cause them to go into system shock.

As well, Emergency dice can be used after cognition based rolls to try them again. These rolls are usually skills such as Investigate, Trivia, Politics, and other knowledge based skills. Emergency dice cannot be used like this for action rolls such as shooting, jumping, or dialogue.

Emergency Dice can also be used, in an emergency, as a consumable resource to give to the GM to alter an outcome, learn something new, or more as the GM offers. This is dependent on the GM, and may not be used at all during a campaign.

# **`Dice Rolling and Tests`**

The system uses the traditional six-sided die when making any test. In a skill or attribute test, the number of six-sided dice rolled is equal to the skill level of the character involved. When two or more dice are rolled simultaneously, their results are not added together. Instead, the highest value rolled is considered to be the outcome of the die roll. If a character’s skill is zero, or modifiers alter the amount of dice rolled to be less than zero, you roll two dice and use the lower number as the outcome before taking into account non-dice maluses or bonuses.

If the outcome of your test is “1”, the result is a Fumble and counts as an overall result of one, plus some unfortunate side effects (see Fumbles, below). The totals of die rolls are often influenced by modifiers, such as Attributes or situation modifiers. 

Modifiers are added to the total of a die roll. If negative modifiers lower the total below zero, the final result is zero.

## **`Taking Average`**

When the character is not in a rush or being hurried or threatened, they are allowed to take 2 plus their skill level as a die result rather than roll for the action the character is attempting. This makes it possible to be automatically successful at tasks that are routine for someone at a given skill level.

## **`Taking High`**

When the character has plenty of time (10 times the normal duration required for a given activity) and is not in a situation where a Fumble would be seriously harmful, They are allowed to take 4 plus the skill level as a die result rather than roll for the action the character is attempting. This allows characters to achieve high end results by spending enough time and effort on them.

## **`Focusing`**

When making a non-opposed action test, a character is allowed to exchange up to 3 dice from their roll for an equivalent bonus to their final result. This can stack with specialization, but you cannot consume E-Dice or any dice gained from bonuses (EG: Dice gained from Rally) in this way.

## **`Fumbles`**

A Fumble is a mistake or mishap that can spell disaster. It is not necessarily caused by the incompetence of the character and may be the result of environmental factors. In general, the lower the skill level, the more likely something will go wrong. A Fumble counts as a result of 1, to which modifiers are applied (which means the test may even succeed, with enough positive modifiers), but something bad happens — the gun jams, the round kick hits the opponent but the character pulls a groin muscle, etc. The GM determines what happens.

## **`Action Tests`** 

Many actions involve an element of chance. In such situations, an Action Test is called for. Action tests consist of a die roll whose result is compared to a fixed difficulty value called a Threshold; a higher Threshold indicates a more difficult situation. 

| Threshold | Type | Example |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Subconscious | Task requiring no thought |
| 2 | Routine | Task requiring little thought |
| 3 | Easy  | Task requiring some attention or skill |
| 4 | Moderate | Task requiring some attention and some skill |
| 5 | Challenging | Task that requires concentration and skill |
| 6 | Difficult | Requires much concentration or proficiency |
| 7 | Very difficult | Requires much concentration and mastery |
| 8 | Extremely Difficult | Absolute focus and mastery |
| 10+ | ‘God please’ | Fight the unbeatable fold. |

If the die roll, with all modifiers (Attribute, situation, gear, etc.) added, surpasses or matches the chosen Threshold, the test succeeds. The degree of success is defined by the Margin of Success (MoS): a value equal to the die roll (plus modifiers) minus the Threshold. The magnitude of the Margin of Success reflects the success of the action test. For example, a MoS of 1 is a marginal success, while a MoS of 6 would be a spectacular success. 

If the die roll, again with modifiers added, is less than the Threshold, the test fails. The degree of failure is defined by the Margin of Failure (MoF): a value equal to the Threshold minus the die roll plus modifiers. A high Margin of Failure indicates a miserably failed action test. For example, a MoF of 1 would be a close call, while a MoF of 6 would be a real (and potentially deadly) failure. If the total die roll and the Threshold are equal, a draw occurs. In role playing situations, draws are often interpreted as marginal successes or ambiguous results.

## **`Opposed Action Tests`**

Sometimes, two individuals will oppose each other’s actions. When two or more individuals oppose each other’s actions, an Opposed Action Test is called for. Each opponent makes a die roll using the appropriate Attribute or skill as Attackers or Defenders. Compare them: the highest result wins the test. The Margin of Success of an Opposed Action Test is equal to the winner’s roll minus the loser’s roll. If more than two participants are involved, separate Margins of Success are worked out between each of the participants as needed. 

Tied results are draws; draws are wins for the Defender.

## **`Skill/Attribute Tests`**

The skill test is the most common form of action test in the game. The number of dice rolled in a skill or attribute test is equal to the level of the skill or attribute being used for that particular test. The appropriate modifiers (Normal and dice bonus and maluses) are added to the result. 

## **`Accumulated Successes`**

Some tasks require a long time and some effort to accomplish. While a very skilled person could conceivably pull it off in one skill test, it is very unlikely (and downright impossible for some tasks, such as designing a new aircraft). Extended tasks will have not only an associated Threshold, but also an associated MoS requirement. For instance, dislodging a stuck bulkhead might require a Body test versus 6, with an associated MoS requirement of 10\. Each successful test budges the bulkhead a little and opens it completely once ten points of MoS are accumulated. Many tasks fit this rule, especially when time or a measure of progress is critical: hunting through a database/file cabinet for a specific piece of information, hotwiring a car, opening a lock when under fire, etc. 

# **`Initiative`**

Initiative determines who acts first each combat round and is used to sort combatants into **Combat Batches**. All combatants roll an opposed Combat Sense test, no Margin of Success is used, only the raw result.

Within each party, combatants are grouped into **Combat Batches** of up to four, ordered by their combat sense result (highest first). Turns alternate between parties, batch by batch (Party A's first batch, then Party B's first batch, then Party A's second batch, and so on). If more than two parties or factions are involved, the same alternating structure applies across all of them. When a combat batch is eliminated or removed from the initiative queue, the queue doesn't change but skips them.

Some enemies may be able to be part of more than one combat batch depending on the circumstances.

**Ties**: If two batches facing off are tied, the parties flip a coin to determine who goes first between them — but the overall alternating turn order is otherwise preserved. This applies at every tied batch-pairing that occurs, not just the first.

**Fumbles**: A character who fumbles their Combat Sense test is confused by the panic and chaos of the moment they cannot take any action that round except Movement. They may still continue an action begun in a previous round that requires multiple rounds to complete (e.g. an accumulated check).

**High rolls:** A character who rolls 6+ on their Initiative test gains an extra Standard Action that round. This action is not affected by, or causes **MAP**

**Guaranteed initiative**: A party benefiting from an ambush, a gadget or grenade effect, or stealth always acts first in the initiative order; they still roll Combat Sense as normal (for batch ordering and other effects), but they will always have the first combat batch.

If two parties both have guaranteed initiative against a shared third party, they flip a coin to determine order between themselves. If two parties both have guaranteed initiative against each other, the effect is negated and they roll combat sense against one another as usual.

# **`Combat`**

During Combat, and on their turn, characters may choose to perform multiple actions out of the action pool of one **Standard Action**, a **Bonus Action**, and **Movement**. Taking multiple actions that require tests in a turn can incur a **Multi Action Penalty** 

The **Multi Action Penalty (MAP)** occurs when a character initiates a test from both their bonus and standard actions in the same turn, whichever action is taken second incurs a \-1 malus to all rolls related to it.

This malus applies and is only caused from tests the character chooses to make. It does not apply to tests prompted or forced by an external source (e.g. Suppression, Movement) and opposed checks made as the defender.

# **`Action Types`**

Actions are split into 3 categories **Standard**, **Bonus**, and **Movement**

### **`Main Actions`**

**Standard** actions include things such as **shooting**, **throwing** grenades, or **readying** yourself are your standard offensive or defensive actions. These actions are affected by movement, which will apply a negative modifier depending on the movement speed. These actions can only be done once per turn, and are usually action, or opposed action tests of some kind. These actions can also be used as **Bonus Actions**

**Bonus** actions include **Deploying**, **Patching Up**, or **Mounting** which are not as extensive as standard actions. These actions usually don't require a roll depending on what they are, and will usually if not always succeed unless something impedes them. 

**Movement** actions allow a character to move across a space, over a specific amount of range bands. Depending on the distance, it may consume your **Bonus** or **Standard** action. Movement may cause tests depending on what the character is trying to do, such as leap across a gap, or climb up a wall.

### **`Unique Actions`**

**Eyes Up**, a miscellaneous action for anything not described by the normal actions and is listed as a bonus action. But can also become a standard action depending on what the GM rules for the specifics.

**Readying:** is a Standard Action and allows the character to do something even after their turn is over. It is essentially a way of holding one’s action until a specified event occurs. An example of readying is, “I draw my gun and will shoot anything that comes out of that doorway.” Should someone walk out of the doorway, before that person can perform any actions the readied character may fire at them. If the turn ends and the Readied Action does not come up, a character may choose to stay readied into the next round, or exit the readied state and make a standard action as normal. A character can remain with their Readied Action for a maximum of five turns.

# **`Standard Actions`**

### **`Shoot`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\]\[Shoot\]**  
Fire away.

Shoot your selected weapon, reducing rounds from the magazine and your total ammo count.

Shooting is an opposed action test between an attacker's respective weapon skill \+ bonuses, and the defender's armor class \+ bonuses.

Abides by ranged combat rules. You can peek 1 hex in any direction to get line of sight on a target.

### **`Throw`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\]\[Throw\]**  
Throw something hard.

Throw a grenade or a throwable object/gadget at an enemy, reduces gadget/grenade count if throwing a grenade or gadget.

Throwing at an individual is usually an opposed action test between an attacker's throw skill and a defender's acrobatics skill, or any other skill depending on the specific grenade or item thrown. Upon success, the defender is able to get out of the way, but is forced out of cover for the rest of the turn. If they have an MOS less than 2 with their result, they are knocked **Prone**

### **`Close Combat`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\] \[CQC\]**  
Get up close and personal.

Attackers get 2 CQC actions per turn including attacking normally. If the attacker lands both hits in their turn, they get a third action for free. This attack can be either **undodgeable** or **unblockable**.

Attackers can also make a heavy attack via applying a \-\[x\] dice to their roll in exchange for adding \[x\] to their damage if landed.

All attacks with hands are CQC, all attacks utilizing an object as a weapon are Melee.

CQC attacks inflict **Temporary Wounds** that can be removed using Recover, and are removed upon exiting combat.

### **`Ready`**

Tags: **\[Support\]**  
Ready yourself for a trigger.

When readying you can hold an action until a specified event occurs. Such as a door opening, an enemy becoming visible or stepping into place, etc. Once declared with a trigger, or the specified event, the character will remain readied until the trigger happens or they leave the readied state.

When readied, you will enact the action even out of your own turn.

### **`Intimidate`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\]**  
Intimidate your foes or others for compliance.

When Intimidating, you roll your intimidation against an enemy's spirit. If you win the opposed roll, you apply your margin of success as a debuff to their actions against for a turn. If you lose, you get the enemy's ire, and they will focus on you.

Only comes into effect if roleplayed out.

### **`Disengage`**

Tags: **\[Defensive\]**  
Disengage from a fight and slip away.

When disengaging, do an opposed test against an enemy's alertness using your stealth skill. Upon success, you are disengaged. Enemies will search your last known position, and in combat it makes enemies unaware of you, but any offensive action will re-engage you.

When moving while disengaged, you will roll stealth tests against an enemy's alertness to remain disengaged and disguise tests if remaining still. Upon firing on an enemy or failing a test, you re-engage with them.

You can also search for disengaged enemies using **Eyes Up**, and prompt a notice test against a defender’s Disguise test.

### **`Rally`**

Tags: **\[Support\] \[Leadership\]**  
Rally your team and other allies.

Once per turn a team member can rally, using a leadership test to bolster the morale of friendlies. The bonus thresholds are:

\<= 1 : no bonuses  
2-4 : \+1 bonus to all friendly actions for the next turn.  
5: \+1 dice bonus to all friendly actions the next turn.  
6+: \+1 dice bonus and \+1 bonus  all friendly actions for the next turn.

Each team member can rally once per encounter. Effect lasts one turn once used. 

However, you can also use rally on a specific friendly target, giving them a callout or command that applies the same effect to them. This can be done multiple times in an encounter and follows the same curve.

Only comes into effect if roleplayed out.

### **`Triple Time`**

Tags: **\[Movement\]**  
Consume your standard action for another instance of movement (2 range bands), allowing you to move in a deadprint towards a destination.

Can only do bonus actions once you arrive at your destination- and with a \-2 malus to all actions done during movement.

# **`Bonus Actions`**

### **`Eyes Up`**

Tags: **\[Misc\] \[Environmental\] \[Opportunistic\]**  
Do an action not encompassed by any standard one. The specific effects and actual action are determined by the GM.

### **`Brace`**

Tags: **\[Defensive\] \[Stance\]**  
Brace yourself against cover.

When bracing, you get an extra \+1 bonus to your armor rolls, however, it reduces your ability to move by one range band the next time you move. Negated by high penetration rifles and a ping.

Can be used out of turn as a reaction, and applies the maluses immediately on your next turn.

### **`Mount`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\] \[Stance\]**  
Mount your rifle against cover

When mounted onto cover, you get an extra \+1 to your weapon rolls. However, mounting restricts your movement by 1 range band until the turn after you unmount or leave cover.

### **`Prone`**

Tags: **\[Defensive\] \[Stance\]**  
Get supine/on your belly or mount your rifle on some cover.

While prone you gain the bonuses of being Mounted as well as a \+1 bonus to stealth rolls if moving, or a \+1 bonus to disguise if still. When prone, the distance you can cover is reduced by one range band

You make little noise while prone, and can easily sneak under vehicles and into vents if need be.

However, you also suffer a \-3 to all \[CQC\] and \[Melee\] rolls, and a \-2 to all movement related rolls \[Acrobatics\].

### **`Deploy`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\]**  
Deploy or use a gadget.

Certain gadgets may end your turn immediately.

### **`Reload`**

Tags: **\[Support\]**  
Reload your selected weapon, restoring your round count to full.

When reloading with ammunition in the mag, it will apply a \-1 malus to your shot.

Reloading from empty applies a \-1 to all actions that turn.

### **`Double Time`**

Tags: **\[Offensive\] \[Movement\]**  
Go the extra mile.

Allows you consume your bonus action to move an extra range band (up to long range) in a pinch at the cost of a compounding \-1 malus to all rolls taken during or after moving.

### **`Patch Up/Repair`**

Tags: **\[Support\] \[Medical\] \[Tinker\]\[Repair Tool\]**  
Patch yourself or someone else up. Or repair vehicles, equipment, and drones

Patching yourself or someone else up requires having 1 FAK for flesh wounds, and 2 FAKs or an AFAK for deep wounds. When consumed, these items are removed from your inventory.

You remove 1+ \[Medical skill result\] divided by 2, rounded down worth of wounds when consumed.

When repairing, roll with tinker to determine the amount of wounds of damage repaired. Remove 1+ \[Medical skill result\] divided by 2, rounded down worth of critical damage when consumed. Prioritizes Critical Damage first, and then fixes equivalent minor damage. Vehicle crews 'relax' when their vehicles are being repaired. Abides by the same rules as healing wounds.

For drones or specific parts that use HP, multiply the result by 3 for how much HP is repaired.

# **`Movement Actions`**

### **`Move/Sprint`**

Tags: **\[Movement\]**  
Move within one range band (Close Range).

While moving, if you do any actions you will take a \-1 to all rolls because of it. If using AC0 armor, this is negated.

You can also Sprint up to two range bands (Medium Range) at a blistering pace.

You can do actions while sprinting, but not too well. you suffer a \-1 to all actions while sprinting. If using AC0 armor, this is reduced to 0\.

### **`Prone`**

Tags: **\[Movement\] \[Stance\]**  
Get supine/on your belly or mount your rifle on some cover.

While prone you gain the bonuses of being Mounted as well as a \+1 bonus to stealth rolls if moving, or a \+1 bonus to disguise if still. Alongside these, when rolling against explosives you gain a \+1 bonus.

The distance you can cover is reduced by one range band.

You make little noise while prone, and can easily sneak under vehicles and into vents if need be.

However, you also suffer a \-3 to all defensive close combat rolls, and a \-2 to all movement related rolls \[Acrobatics\].

### **`Mantle`**

Tags: **\[Movement\] \[Elevation\]**  
Mantle onto cover

When mantling, you are able to increase your elevation, or decrease it by jumping down to a lower level. To do so, make a jump test against 2+ your armor class. On success, you reach the elevation you want to be at. Upon failure, you fall and abide by falling rules. If the fall causes no wounds, you become **winded** instead, taking a \-1 malus to your next action. 

When mantling onto cover, you make an action test against 2+ your armor class. Mantling onto standard cover or a vehicle grants a transition-level elevation boost and functions as a transition between elevations while also allowing you to shoot over tall cover. Mantling onto Tall Cover grants a full elevation bump to the next elevation.

While mantled, you are no longer considered in cover; you cannot be shielded by the object you're standing on top of. You may still gain cover from Tall Cover if you’re on top of normal cover, or a soft or hard wall if on top of tall cover, in a hex adjacent to what you're standing on (e.g. mantled on a car being used as cover, with a Tall Cover wall in the adjacent hex you can peek over). 

# **`Movement Rules`**

### **`Movement`**

Movement is when a character is changing positions during a turn. Stances can affect how far a character can move during a turn, and are covered elsewhere. Every character can move up to 2 range bands freely unless impeded by an affliction (eg: Suppression, Debilitated, or Crippled) or in a specific stance that reduces how many range bands they can move. A character will move from point A to point B over a course of a turn- if they choose to stop in the middle to make an action as they are moving to point B, they incur Movement Maluses to any tests made.

**Movement Maluses** occur when a character does an action that requires them to stop and start moving again, or takes an action while moving, any tests related to that action takes a \-1 malus. This rule applies uniformly to every movement instance.

**Double Time** is a specific bonus action that can be made during a character’s turn. This provides them one extra range band during an instance of movement. However, this will apply a \-1 malus to all tests that are taken during that movement, or after you’ve reached your destination. This compounds with the standard movement maluses.

**Triple Time** is a specific Standard Action that can be taken during a character's turn. This provides a separate, extra instance of movement after stopping (2 range bands). This applies a \-2 malus to all actions taken during that instance of movement, which compounds with the standard movement malus. This malus does not apply to actions taken after this instance of movement ends (e.g. from an extra Standard Action gained via Initiative or stims). 

**Range Bands** describes a specific distance from a character. Each band is 8 hexes long, with the fourth and fifth bands being an ‘unknown’ distance due to their nature.

\- *Close range* is 8 hexes from a character. Denoted as **C**  
*\- Medium range* is 16 hexes from a character. Denoted as **M**  
\- *Long range* is 24 hexes from a character. Denoted as **L**  
\- *Extreme Long Range* is past 24 hexes from a character to the edge of the map. Denoted as **ELR**   
\- *Extreme, Extreme Long Range* is anywhere outside of the map, and in another world hex. This is specific to *Grand Operations*, covered elsewhere. And is denoted as **EELR**

| Action | Distance Covered | Action Cost | Maluses |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Move** | 1 range band (Close, 8 hexes) | Free unless impeded | None |
| **Sprint** | 2 range bands (Medium, 16 hexes) | Free unless impeded | \-1 malus during movement |
| **Double Time** | 1 extra range band | Bonus Action | \-1 malus during and after movement |
| **Triple Time** | A second instance of movement of up to 2 more range bands | Standard Action | \-2 malus during movement |

### **`Stances`**

There are four stances a character can be in: **Standing**, **Prone**, **Bracing**, and **Mounted**. Stances represent a character's positioning and persist between turns until changed. If changed from one stance to another involuntarily, such as from being tripped in CQC or succeeding an acrobatics test with an MOS  less than 2 against a grenade, you don't incur the maluses for leaving your prior stance.

**Standing** is when the character is upright, behind cover, standing normally, or otherwise unencumbered in a way that doesn't restrict movement. This is the normal stance a character usually would be in unless specified.

**Prone** is when a character is lying flat on the ground, either supine or on their stomach. This offers concealment and a lower profile, but comes with a \-2 malus to movement tests (eg: Acrobatics, Jump) while prone, as well as a \-3 malus to defensive CQC actions. When in prone and exiting prone, the total range bands you can move are reduced by 1\. You can freely transition from standing to prone during your movement action.

**Bracing** is when the character is hunkering down and hugging it tightly to avoid shrapnel and rounds. This reduces the total range bands you can move by 1 when you exit this stance, in exchange you get a flat \+1 bonus to your armor test. You cannot be mounted or prone while bracing. Upon moving, you leave this state and return to standing. Requires a bonus action to transition into.

**Mounted** is when the character is steadying or nocking their firearm against a corner or wall. This reduces the total range bands you can move by 1, in exchange you gain a flat \+1 bonus to your shooting test. You cannot be bracing or prone while mounting. Upon moving, you leave this state and return to standing. Requires a bonus action to transition into.

### **`Climbing`**

Climbing is a special movement case, since gravity and skill come into play. In order to move up, down or across a wall or slope, a character must make a successful Climbing skill test. Failure means the character makes no progress that round and stays where they are. A failure with a MoF of 4 or greater, or a Fumble, indicates the character has begun to lose his grip and must spend the next round regaining it (with a successful Climbing test). Once the grip is lost, each additional failed test gives a \-1 modifier to further tests (cumulative). When the character can no longer pass the test (i.e., the Threshold cannot be met in any way), they fall. 

### **`Falling`**

Falling is very damaging to the body. A number of damage equal to the number of meters fallen is gained. This is compared to the Wound Thresholds, like any other attack (see Injuries, in Damage section). For example, falling one meter causes 1 point of damage; falling two meters causes 2 points of damage, and so on. For example, a four-meter fall would cause, using the 4 points of damage. This is a flesh wound for most people, a badly sprained ankle or a broken wrist perhaps.

### **`Jumping`**

To jump, a character must make a Jump test, modified by your movement speed (+0 for normal movement, \+1 for Double Time, \+2 for Triple time). The basic Threshold is 2+ your armor class; this is modified by the condition(s) of the terrain (+3 if rough; \+6 if slippery; \+9 if crumbling; etc.).

### **`Cover`**

Cover allows a character to increase their defensive armor rolls against kinetic damage by a \+1 bonus. They are considered in cover whenever there is something that could be obstructing a shooter's line of sight to them. Such as a corner of a wall, a crate they’re hiding behind, or an engine block. To determine if someone is in cover, draw an imaginary line from the attacker or grenade to the defender. If the line passes over a hex with cover, tall cover, soft walls, or hard walls on it- then the target is in cover. Cover only applies if they can peek out and shoot back at you in some regard, or if using normal cover..

### **`Peeking`**

Peeking allows a character to peek out of cover when firing. When behind something obscuring a character’s view, and if moving one more hex in some direction would give them line of sight- they can quickly peek out into that hex, fire, and return to their original hex. This however still uses the distance from their original hex rather than the distance from the hex they are peeking out from.

### **`Elevation`**

Elevation is measured in **Steps**. Standard ground is 0, and each floor is 2 steps tall, with the next floor starting at its second step. High Ground (teal) is \+2 and Low Ground (green) is \-2. Hexes hashed in an elevation's color are transition hexes and are considered a halfway (±1) between standard ground and that elevation. Cover, walls, and more all adopt the elevation of the area they sit on.

Transition hexes represent stairs, a ladder, or a general incline of some kind. A transition hex allows you to freely move from high to low ground, and from low to normal ground, or vice versa freely as long as you pass through it on the way to the next elevation. Standing on a transition hex is equivalent to being either ± 1 step in elevation depending on if it transitions to low ground (green) or high ground (teal)

The edges of transition hexes count as tall cover to lower elevations. Determine line of sight through and along them as you would for any other piece of tall cover. Seeing a target through transition hexes, to lower ground, negates the lower ground LOS benefits.

A character can also mantle onto cover and tall cover to gain elevation. If a character mantles onto standard cover, they must succeed a Climb test against a threshold of 2 \+ their armor class to gain an extra step of elevation. If they choose to mantle onto a piece of tall cover, they must succeed a Climb test against a threshold of 3 \+ AC to gain 2 steps of  elevation.

If a character tries to descend steps in elevation, they must make a jump test against a threshold of the amount of steps descended \+ their armor class.

Line of sight is restricted only between characters 2 or more steps apart in elevation. Restrictions are mutual: if one character can't see the other, neither can the other. However, there are specific interactions between higher and lower grounds:

**Higher ground:** A character on higher ground and more than 3 hexes from the edge of their elevation is out of line of sight of anyone on lower elevation. Within 3 hexes of the edge, they are visible. This includes an edge that leads into a transition hex.

**Lower ground:** A character on lower ground within 3 hexes of the edge of their elevation (the base of the wall) is out of line of sight of anyone on higher elevation. More than 3 hexes from the edge, they are visible to higher elevations. A trench is narrow enough (3 hexes wide) that everyone would count as hidden unless someone has elevation overmatch on them.

**Elevation Overmatch:** A character who is 2 or more steps above a target and within 2 hexes of the edge of their own elevation can see straight down and ignores the lower-ground restriction. Whenever a character targets someone using Elevation Overmatch, the target's cover bonus is negated.

# **`Injuries`**

## **`Taking Damage`**

Getting shot hurts. As you collect wounds and near a system shock, you become less and less combat effective, making even the action of shooting back become a struggle. However, you can always heal these wounds with all sorts of medical equipment, and the help of a handy-dandy medic nearby. Just don’t get a painkiller addiction. 

Damage is taken as the MOS of an attacker’s test, or the MOF of the character’s defensive test, multiplied by the base damage of whatever is inflicting damage to the character.

When you take an instance of damage, compare it against your thresholds. It meets a threshold if it equals or exceeds it, and you take the wound for the highest threshold met. If the instance of damage does not meet or exceed any of the thresholds, nothing happens and no wounds are taken.

Each instance of damage resolves against your thresholds individually.

**Flesh wound** is a catch-all term for painful but generally non-life- threatening injuries such as minor gunshot wounds, deep cuts or minor concussions. These apply a \-1 malus to all tests that stack for each wound. Your flesh wound threshold is your Stamina divided by 2, then added with your armor class.

**Deep wounds** are more severe injuries: damage to major blood vessels, bad gunshot wounds etc. Deep wounds apply a \-2 malus to all tests that stack with each wound. Depending on what caused the deep wound, and where the deep wound is- a character may be unable to move. Your deep wound threshold is your Stamina added with your armor class.

**Instant Death** are for injuries or damage that don’t just cause at most a deep wound. Instant Death causes a character to go into DBNO, or if they have already gone DBNO three times- become Incapacitated. Your instant death threshold is your Stamina multiplied by 2\.

**System Shock** is the maximum wound modifier you can sustain before going into shock from the trauma. When your wound modifier matches or exceeds your system shock threshold, you enter DBNO. Your system shock threshold is 5 \+ Health

## **`Critical States`**

When someone goes down in a fight, it can either be by taking in instance of damage that meets or passes the Instant Death threshold, or by going into system shock by the amount of wounds taken. If someone takes enough damage to meet their Instant Death Threshold or wounds to meet or exceed their System Shock threshold, they enter into **DBNO**. Or 'Down But Not Out'.

In **DBNO**, characters are down on the ground and are considered prone. They have to make a Body or Spirit test against a threshold of 4 to stay alive on their turn, and upon three failed tests while they are down, they become incapacitated. These failed tests reset when the character is revived. A character also becomes incapacitated upon their third down.

Characters can go straight to incapacitation if melee'd while down as an execution, however when shot while down- it adds a failure that adds to those three failed tests. You don't take damage while down.

When reviving a character from DBNO, an AFAK can be used to revive them and replace all prior wounds with one deep wound (even if they had no wounds before). Medics can also use Shock Nanites to revive a character and replace all prior wounds with one flesh wound, which clears at the start of the character's next turn.

Each time a character goes down- they take a persistent malus.  
\-1st Down: Nothing  
\-2nd Down: \-1 dice malus to all tests.  
\-3rd Down: Incapacitation

These maluses and your downs are reset once you return to base, patch yourself up at a Bivouac, or become incapacitated.

When a character is **incapacitated**, they are incapable of taking any actions and cannot continue the mission without dedicated medical attention from a Medic, or two AFAKs to stabilize them. A character can only be revived once from an incapacitated state during a mission. On revival, all of their wounds are cleared, and they then take wounds similar to DBNO revival (one deep wound from an AFAK, or one flesh wound from Shock Nanites that is removed their next turn). For the rest of the mission they also suffer a persistent deep wound, a permanent \-2 malus to all tests that cannot be cleared by treatment until they return to base. This wound counts toward the character's system shock threshold. Upon a second incapacitation, the character can no longer continue the mission, and may die at the GM's discretion.

## **`Healing and Treatment`**

Medical Treatment is used to clear wounds that have been collected over the course of a mission or fight. To clear wounds you need to take a medical skill test and determine the type of wounds from the item used and the amount of wounds cleared from the result.

A quick reference for how many wounds a result clears is

| Result | Effect |
| :---- | :---- |
| \<2  | 1 wound cleared |
| 3-4 | 2 wounds cleared |
| 5-6  | 3 wounds cleared |
| 7+ | 4 wounds cleared |

You cannot hurt yourself or someone else while using nanites or medical kits. And are always guaranteed to clear at least one wound- even if the result is negative. When healing yourself, you don't include your wound modifier.

There are 3 main methods of treatment  
\-Using dedicated nanite stimulants available to the medic class  
\-Using an AFAK  
\-Using an IFAK

Each of these have different effects, though the specific nanites are covered in the gear section. The IFAK and AFAK are available to everyone.

\-**IFAKs** are single use and clear only flesh wounds, but a character can consume two as part of the same healing action to get the same effect as an AFAK and clear deep wounds.  
\-**AFAKs** are single use, clear deep and flesh wounds, but will prioritize clearing deep wounds first and then clearing the equivalent of flesh wounds after (2 flesh wounds for every deep). 

### **`Wound Overflow`**

Wound Overflow prevents instant death. When a character takes an instance of damage that beats or meets their instant death threshold, and is either from a source with this tag or does an action that causes damage taken to be with wound overflow, they take a deep wound and subtract the deep wound threshold from the total damage. Repeat this as long as the remaining damage still meets that threshold. 

Once it doesn’t, repeat this against the flesh wound threshold and take flesh wounds until the remaining damage is below the flesh wound threshold. Any leftover damage is discarded.

You can still meet or exceed the system shock threshold by wound overflow and enter DBNO.

# **`Ranged Combat`**

To attack a wary target within sight and range, the attacker must defeat the defender in an Opposed Skill test. The attacker rolls their skill in the attack form being used (Light, Heavy, Medium Weapons or Throwing). The defender rolls their armor class. Both should apply modifiers as they've tracked them. IE: Range and Pings for attackers, and cover or concealment for defenders.

Attacking unwary targets requires the attacker to pass a standard (not Opposed) skill test with a Threshold of 2 \+ the defender’s alertness \+ the defender’s modifiers (like cover). To catch a target unaware usually requires some prior planning like laying an ambush or sneaking up on them; targets may make an Opposed Alertness test against the attacker’s Stealth test (minus modifiers for conditions) if they are alert. Success means the target notices the attacker and can take defensive actions as a reaction; this can sometimes happen long before the attacker is ready to fire. 

To attack an inanimate object requires a Skill test with a Threshold equal to 1 plus any applicable modifier. This assumes that the target is man-sized or larger; if not; refer to the aiming rules.

If the attacker wins the test, the attack hits and the damage is multiplied by the MOS. If the defender wins, the attack misses. If it's a tie, the attack connects, but no damage is dealt. If the attacker Fumbles, he jams his weapon (You can unjam via a Reload Bonus Action)

Shooting through smoke and walls is covered in Penetration. However other versions of ranged attacks can be read below: 

### **`Steadying`**

A character can spend up to 3 combat rounds steadying their aim to improve their odds to hit. Add one die to the attacker’s test per round spent steadying, up to a maximum of \+3. The character may not move while Steadying.

When Steadying, you take a \-1 malus to reactions and your awareness, such as getting away from a grenade or noticing someone sneaking up on you. This doesn't apply to the target you are steadying your aim at, if there is one.

### **`Aiming`**

A character can aim at a specific vehicle or body part (e.g. torso, arm or leg, APS, or wheels).

\-Aiming at a torso-sized target removes one die from the skill test.  
\-Aiming at a limb sized target removes two dice from the skill test.  
\-Aiming at a head sized target removes three dice from the skill test.

For torso and limb shots, the target’s wound thresholds are halved (rounded up). Limb shots specifically cause Wound Overflow and not instant death. For headshots, the instant death threshold is also halved- as well as negating the use of E-dice to escape instant death.

You can aim at inanimate objects against a threshold of 2\. 

### **`Bursting`**

Assault rifles and Marksman Rifles are capable of burst fire. Burst attacks usually consist of firing a tight burst towards a single opponent. The attacker may decrease a number of his dice, up to a maximum of \-2.

If the attack succeeds, the amount of dice decreased is added to the attack’s Margin of Success, increasing the damage. 

### **`Spraying`**

SMGs and Carbines are capable of rapid, automatic fire that can be used to attack multiple targets in a single action by spraying across targets. Thus, people or objects between the targets can be hit as well. A player must declare that they are spraying before any attacks are made. The player then chooses the targets of his attack.

The maximum number of targets that possibly can be attacked is three. All targets must be no more than five hexes apart (if they are, count each set of hexes as a target or every 5m gap). Each attack is rolled separately, and each individual target can be attacked more than once per round by the same weapon. This attack consumes the same amount of ammo as a normal firing action, multiplied by the amount of times fired. 

### **`Suppressing`**

Suppression is unique to machine guns and allows them to put lots of lead in the air to make others keep their head down. The attacker chooses a target, draws a line from the attacker to the defender, then rolls their attack normally and marks down 2 turns of ammo instead of 1\. Record the result and add 2 to it: that’s the Saturation Fire Threshold. Anyone or anything which enters within one hex of the line of fire or the target during the round must beat the Saturation Threshold or take damage. Anyone within one hex of the line of fire, along with one hex around the defender, are considered Suppressed.

When Suppressed, offensive actions such as shooting, deploying drones, or throwing grenades will cause an armor test against the saturation fire threshold for damage.

If a defender isn't in cover, they are immediately shifted to prone.

When Suppressed, any character not in cover from the source of suppression is immediately knocked prone. At the start of their turn, they must pass a Spirit test against a threshold of 4 or take a \-1 dice malus to all tests and lose the ability to move or make offensive actions for the turn.

If the suppressed character makes an offensive action, they make an armor test against the saturation fire threshold for damage. A character can leave suppression when they leave the line of fire from the source of suppression.

Attackers can split their fire towards a maximum of 3 targets, however this will divide the threshold by how many targets there are. If damage is taken, or they are interrupted by items such as a flashbang, suppression is stopped. 

### **`Extreme Long Range Shooting`**

When shooting from extreme long range and from 'out of map' (As in you are firing from an adjacent hex over as a sniper) you have much more factors to consider when shooting. Humidity, Elevation, Travel Time, and even the spin of the earth via the coriolis effect can change the impact point of your shot at those ranges. As such, before firing from EELR and out of Hex, you need to make a navigate test to determine how well your character has accounted for these factors before the shot and how they will affect it.

\>1 \[-2\] is a complete failure or miscalculation, \-2 malus  
\>2-3 \[-1\] is succeeding in 'some' of the calculations and adjusting for those factors, \-1 malus  
\>4-5 \[+0\] is accurately accounting for factors and leads to no maluses  
\>6+ \[+1\] is spot on and extremely accurate with your calculations, \+1 bonus.

# **`Close Combat`** 

To affect a wary target, the attacker must defeat the defender in opposed tests. The attacker uses their skill depending on what they are using (Melee or CQC). The defender either Blocks (uses Body), Parries (uses matching skill), or Dodges (Uses Athletics). Attackers should add the appropriate modifiers to their roll depending upon environmental and other circumstances. 

On each character's turn they are considered attacking. When attacking they get two CQC actions, and if they land two attacks they get a third action that can be either **unblockable** or **undodgeable**. The following actions and mechanics for attacking and defending are listed further below 

Attacking unwary targets requires the attacker to pass a standard (not opposed) skill test with a Threshold number of 2 plus the defender's alertness. However, unlike a normal attack, the attacker must roll the lower of two skills: either his chosen attack skill or his Stealth skill. Untrained individuals tend to be noisy or otherwise mess up surprise attacks by alerting the victim at the last moment.

To attack an inanimate object requires a skill test with a Threshold equal to 1 plus any applicable modifier. This assumes that the target is man-sized or larger; if not, refer to the Aiming rule. Inanimate objects don't get to make any actions.

### **`Disengaging`**

When someone attempts to disengage from CQC, The opponent is entitled to a successful grapple test where the character disengaging is the defender. If the attacker succeeds, they are given a punish, if the defender succeeds, they are able to move freely. If they have one CQC Action remaining after leaving, they will still have their standard action and movement, but no bonus action. If they don't have any CQC actions remaining, they exit CQC with only a bonus action and their movement. Disengaging from CQC is considered a CQC action.

### **`Punishes`**

Punishing allows the Defender to immediately return an attack and end the attacker's turn. This attack has a \-1 to the defender's check and isn't counted in the 2 standard CQC Actions a character would have in a turn. 

### **`Focusing`**

Focusing allows the Attacker and Defender to reduce their dice for a normal bonus to your test when in CQC, abiding by the same rules as focusing for non-combat tests.

### **`Grappling`**

To Grapple an opponent both characters make an opposed action test using their CQC skill.. If the attacker wins, the attacker has the defender in a grapple to varying degrees of control and can make an extra action.

MOF 2+: Whiff, Defender punishes the attacker.  
MOF 1-0: Bunt, no extra action.  
MOS 1/2: Partial, attacker can shove as a follow up action.  
MOS 3+: Full, attack/shove (now is a guaranteed knockdown)

When grappled, defenders have a \-1 to all defending for that turn until let go. Characters who are grappled can leave the grapple through succeeding an opposed grapple test.

If a character moves within 2 hexes of another character not in CQC with a separate character, they are entitled to attempt to grapple them as a bonus action. However, you move to their position immediately and it can drag you out of cover. Upon MOS of 2+, you can drag them towards you instead. 

### **`Disarming`**

Attacker CQC/Melee vs Defender Alertness. The attacker must have at least 2 MoS to succeed.  
If the defender succeeds, nothing happens. If the attacker succeeds, the weapon is knocked out of the defender's hands. If using CQC, the weapon is now in the attackers hands 

### **`Tripping`**

Opposed roll, Attacker CQC vs Defender Notice and Acrobatics.

The Defenders rolls notice first, if it succeeds against the attack, then the defender rolls acrobatics to dodge.

MOS of 1 applies a \-1 to defender's next action or check (Off Stance)  
MOS of 2+ knocks the opponent down (prone). 

### **`Shoving`**

Opposed roll, Attacker CQC vs Defender Body to resist.  
Shoves the defender up to 3+ body hexes away. If the Defender is knocked into wall/cover, they receive a \-1 malus on their next defending action. If the attack is from a full control shove, it’s an immediate knockdown.

if MOS \>2 or in a grapple with full control, the opponent is knocked down (prone). 

### **`Recover`**

Recover allows the user to either restore temporary wounds, get up from being prone, or improve their defensive stance. 

To restore temporary wounds:

Roll Body without any maluses. Recover a temporary deep wound, or temporary flesh equivalent based off of the result of your roll on the curve of:

1-3 \= 1 Deep wounds  
4-6 \= 2 Deep wounds

And then end the turn immediately.

To get up from being prone: Consumes one CQC action.

To improve your defensive stance: Consume one CQC action, allowing the defender to improve your next defensive check by 1 dice. 

### **`Dodge`**

Opposed roll, Defender Athletics vs Attacker roll. 

Dodging allows the defender to avoid a direct enemy attack, every dodge after the first will incur a compounding \-1 malus until you do a Recovery action.

Converts all attacks to flesh wounds if MOF less than 3\. MOS of 2+ results in a defender punish. 

### **`Block`**

Opposed roll, Defender Body roll vs Attacker action (melee, cqc, etc)  

Every block after the first will incur a compounding \-1 malus until you Recover.

Always reduces opponent MOS by 1\. Negates instant death even when failed, but causes Wound Overflow instead. 

### **`Parry`**

Opposed roll, Defender matching stat vs Attacker action (melee, cqc, etc)

Every Parry after the first will incur a compounding \-1 malus until you Recover.

Parrying requires an MOS of 2 to succeed, if it fails, the attack goes through with at least MOS of 1\. If successful, immediately ends the Attackers turn and results in a punish.

# **`Stealth`**

Tests for detection are done by rolling an attacker’s Stealth against a threshold of 2+ the defender’s alertness. These tests can also be rolled with Disguise if using camouflage or holding still, like hiding in a bush or covering yourself in mud or snow to blend in with it. Lighting can also affect stealth, as poor lighting conditions is a \+1 to your roll, very poor lighting is \+2, and perfect darkness is \+3. Stance and cover also affects your stealth, as crawling and using cover can increase your bonus by \+1 each.

If attempting to sneak past, or enter an area surreptitiously, you only need to roll stealth once against a defender's alertness for them not to notice you as you move for subsequent turns. However, when interacting with locks, computers, or anything else in that vein, the attacker must roll their stealth value as well as the respective skill against the defender’s alertness for that action. Fumbles while in stealth will cause guards to get suspicious and come investigate.

However, stealth is not a binary switch between ‘not alert’ and ‘alert’. Enemies will be in a set of states depending on the conditions. When unaware. Enemies are inattentive and will have a harder time to notice you. However, as further actions such as guards going missing, objects being found tampered with, and possible evidence of intruders come in- enemies will start to become more and more alert. This is represented by various states. 

**\[Contact\]**  
Contact implies that enemies are present, but not fully aware of your presence. They are at best inattentive and relaxed, and don't suspect intruders to be right at their gates. In this state, enemies are more likely to check quickly and return to their patrol routes or whatever they were doing prior, usually making a spot check of an area to ensure things ‘seem’ in order. However, as disturbances keep cropping up, enemies will start to become more and more attentive of minor changes. An open door, a brass casing on the ground, a light that wasn’t on a few minutes ago, or even an odd gust of wind from a window that got opened on entry. In this stage, cutting a guard’s transmission or an HVT’s phone call is a quick way to escalate to a higher alert status.

In this state, the enemy detection threshold is reduced by 1\.

Covering your tracks is the best way to remain at this state of alertness, but eventually even the smaller things will add up. 

**\[Local Alert\]**  
In this state enemies in the area are alert, just not alert specifically to you. They know something is amiss, and are trying to find out what. Any disturbances will turn into more thorough checks, and so on and so forth. However, enemies won't report this into the rest of the base, and instead will investigate things themselves. But- if they find something that's certain evidence, they’ll report it in. 

If nothing is found for 3 turns, the state reverts to **\[Contact\]**, however- enemies who are suspicious will remain on alert.

**\[Alert\]**  
In this state, enemies are on alert and actively searching. Patrols are much more attentive to small disturbances, and personnel within the base are tasked to help with search efforts. In this state HVTs may move towards safer areas, and guards will begin to group up for power in numbers. If players are spotted, and they are allowed to put out an alert to the rest of the base- enemies will begin to rush to support troops in contact.

In this state, enemies make opposed notice tests against your stealth or disguise and will spend time searching for you.

**\[Compromised\]**  
When compromised, enemies are aware of the team and are calling in reinforcements to support. By this point stealth has gone out the window, and the team needs to fight through, or disengage. This is also where combat properly begins.

# **`Vehicle Combat`**

At select points throughout a campaign, the party may come across encounters fighting against vehicles of various forms. These encounters will require players to use their resources and pick their equipment wisely as certain vehicle types have a limited set of weaknesses. Light armored vehicles like Jeeps and civilian cars may be damaged by sustained rifle fire, whereas heavy armor such as a tank may take multiple RPG rounds to bring down.

Certain vehicle types will also have countermeasures that players will have to keep track of and learn to counter as they play, ensuring they will ultimately prevail against overwhelming odds. 

DM discretion may also allow players to pull off more risky and creative moves to take out an enemy vehicle. For example, if a player wants to try and climb aboard an enemy tank and drop a grenade in its hatch, the DM may ask for them to make an athletics check to pass.

Vehicle health is split into 2 systems. The standard wound and injury system for the overall vehicle health and systems, and the HP system for individual vehicle parts. Wounds for vehicles work the same for infantry, however they apply a malus for all actions done using the vehicle and can stack with individual crew wounds if they have any.

HP damage works by taking the total resolved damage, and then subtracting it from the HP of the component. Damage to components won't cause damage or wounds to the vehicle itself, but it will cause damage to the component and disable things on the vehicle. Such as their APS, their tracks, and more. 

In addition, some vehicles have AT instead of AC for their armor values. AT is unique in that it can only be affected by weapons with penetration values of 3 and 4, such as RPGs and AMRs. If no specific AT or AC is listed, the component adopts the armor class of the base vehicle.

The skills of the vehicle are derived from the occupants and the crew as a whole. If inside a ground vehicle, as a bonus action you can move from one position to another, or also dismount.

Vehicles can move up to three range bands freely, as well as traverse tough terrain without any issues. Similarly, climbing on top of a vehicle provides a step of elevation upwards.

The AOO of a vehicle determines how much space it occupies on the map. AOO of 0 occupies only the hex it is on, AOO of 1 occupies a 1 hex AOE, while AOO of 2 occupies an AOE of 2 from the hex the vehicle is on. AOO also counts as part of the vehicle, and is considered cover for infantry unless it is an airborne vehicle.

When a vehicle enters system shock or is hit with enough damage to surpass the instant death threshold, it is rendered disabled. Depending on the GM, the damage inflicted may not kill the crew, or immediately kill the crew upon surpassing the instant death threshold. If a vehicle enters the system shock threshold, it will never kill the crew and the vehicle is recoverable. If a character has an E-dice, upon a vehicle they are occupying taking damage to cause instant death, they can instead mark 2 deep wounds and eject themselves from the vehicle.

# **`Rotor Wing Aircraft`**

Rotor Wing Aircraft are unique in that they are airborne instead of on the ground. As such- they have a unique mechanic to switch between **High Altitude** and **Low Altitude** during their movement. At both altitudes, they can ignore cover, tall cover, terrain, and anything else that can impede movement on the ground.

High altitude allows them to gain an extra range band to all targets and a \+1 to tests against unguided munitions to dodge them, as well as ignoring all terrain and being free to move with the caveat that they cannot fire into buildings from above. However, at high altitude, they can be targeted by fixed wing aircraft and AA systems outside of the map easier

At low altitude, they are closer to the ground and lose high altitude benefits, however cannot be easily targeted by AA from outside of the map and gain a \+1 to defensive tests against fixed wing aircraft. At low altitude, they can fire into buildings, however cannot enter buildings or ignore soft and hard walls.

\[M-Wep\]: Missiles and Rockets

# **`CAS and Artillery`**

When calling in CAS for aviation, the person requesting rolls their navigate skill on a curve that affects the aircraft's roll against a threshold of 3\. If you fail, the aircraft suffers a \-1 to their roll. When using a SOFLAM as a rifleman, success adds a \+1 to the roll, as well as making the strike occur immediately from aircraft no matter the success or failure. Helicopters are always immediate at the cost of being able to be shot at and marked on the map.

When calling in Fire Support from Ground and Naval sources- the person requesting makes the same roll with the same effects. However, the munitions will always take one turn to arrive, and always arrive at the start of the requester's next turn due to the distance.

## **`Close Air Support`**

Aircraft and Helicopters have a certain capacity for attacks that determines how many or each munition they have before they need to leave and re-arm. Re-arming is determined by how much runway they need, allowing them to either re-arm from dedicated airbases, forward strips, or FARPs (Specific to VTOL and Rotor Wing aircraft). Capturing one during Grand Operations allows you to sortie aircraft from that position- and can exponentially speed up the re-arm speed (in turns) as determined before a mission by the GM.

Aircraft also have core attributes to help determine their wound thresholds, and attacking rolls.

\-Alertness represents their MAWS, RWR, and other defensive systems, along with the reflex of the pilot.  
\-Body is the structural rigidity, as well as the amount of G-force or damage a pilot can take  
\-Intelligence is the skill and experience of the pilot with the airframe  
\-Spirit represents the pilot's confidence in the aircraft and controlling their nerves under pressure

When attacking, pilots roll Intelligence. When defending against ground fire from infantry or AA guns, they roll Spirit. However, when rolling against guided systems such as SAM launches or enemy air launched missiles, they first roll alertness against a threshold of 3\. Upon failure- they receive a \-1 to their Spirit roll.

# **`Armor`**

The human body is a fragile thing, one bullet in the right place can do enough damage to maim someone for life. Which is why armor is always important.

Armor in gameplay mainly focuses on the coverage provided. Where AC0 has no armor at all, just a chest rig at best, and AC2 includes a groin flap \+ deltoid armor and optionally comes with a ballistic visor. Your armor value follows the same rules as skill tests, and is the amount of die you roll as a defender in ranged combat. AC0 rolls two dice and uses the lower value as your result.

What level of armor also affects how encumbered you are as a person, where:

| Armor Values |  |  |  |

| AC | Description |  |  |
| **AC0** | No armor. Chest rig or just plainclothes.  Available to all classes.No maluses to actions after or during movement. \+1 bonus to movement related tests \[Acrobatics\]\[Endurance\]\[Jump\]\[Climb\] \+1 bonus to \[Stealth\] tests. |  |  |
| **AC1** | Plate carrier \+ helmet. Basic coverage. Available to all classes. No effects. |  |  |

You can always choose to strip yourself of armor if need be. However, you will always reduce to AC0 when you strip, and then return to your earlier armor class when you re-armor. These are both counted as a \[Deploy\] action.

# **`Weapons`**

As a member of a premier PMC with leading military R\&D, characters get access to a wide range of weapons to use against their many foes. Weapons in Calamari are divided across weapon classes, each weapon class has a universal statistic associated with it allowing players to select whatever weapon of their choosing that fits into that weapon class and simply copy the stats. Within those classes are weapon families that can alter the specific stats or functions of a weapon. Family costs are added onto the base weapon’s costs when purchased.

Weapon stats are formatted where **PEN** is a weapon’s penetration value, **DMG** is the base damage a weapon will do to targets before modifiers and MOS, **RSV** is the total turns of ammo a weapon has, and **CAP** is how many turns are in a magazine.

## **`Ammo`**

Ammo is handled in turns of ammunition- where the total reserve ammo is how many turns of ammo you have for that weapon in total, and the turns per magazine are the amount of turns you can fire until empty. When firing, mark a turn of ammunition spent from your magazine and your total reserves, and keep track of how many you have left in the magazine. 

Reloading allows you to restock the turns of ammo in your magazine. When you reload, reset the turns of ammo in your weapon’s magazine to its capacity. Reloading from empty applies a \-1 to all actions that turn, while reloading with ammo left in the magazine will apply a \-1 malus to your shooting test. If you run out of ammo in your magazine- you cannot make a shooting action with that weapon.

## **`Penetration`**

Penetration values are weapon stats that help determine just how much a gun can punch through. For the sake of gameplay, Body armor can tank most rounds within ‘reason’ and are equivalent to Level IV. Someone is obviously not surviving being hit by a tank round or a .50 BMG to the chest very easily.

Penetration can be used to hit targets behind cover and walls according to the following rules:  
\- When you don't have an LOS on a target, you are shooting with a 'Very Dimly Lit' modifier (-2).  
\- Upon a successful notice roll for where they may be, reduce the effect to just 'poorly Lit' (-1).  
\- Pinged enemies have no negative modifier when shooting through walls.  
\- If they are pinged and the weapon will penetrate a target's cover, it negates the cover bonus.

Penetration values of 3+ are able to affect and damage vehicles directly. Such as AMRs, Rocket launchers, etc.

Penetration Values are as follows:  
**PEN 1**: Penetrates everything up to but excluding cover. Includes soft walls.  
**PEN 2**: Penetrates thick cover up to but excluding hard walls and metal. Includes most cover.  
**PEN 3**: Penetrates hard walls and up to thick-well reinforced materials and vehicle armor.  
**PEN 4**: Penetrates everything up to structures, consecutive walls, and terrain.

If your cover is destroyed after the turn- you do not retain the bracing bonus afterward.

# *`Gear Slots`**

Beyond standard-issue weapons and consumables, characters can also buy into gearsets developed and licensed by private manufacturers tailored towards specific use cases, alongside more general equipment to fill the 4 gear slots a character has:

**Headgear**: Affects vision, datalink, and focuses on improving your ARD to provide extra bonuses. See and connect with more.

**Vest**: Affects capacity, armor, and what you can carry. Carry better gear, more gear, different guns, or be harder to kill.

**Gloves**: Affects your ability to manipulate and interface with the world and gear you have. Tinker with items, shoulder your weapons faster, grip things harder.

**Equipment :** Affects your equipment and gadgets directly by giving them more ammo or changing how they work. Shoot fire, rain lead, carry a third gun.

**Patch:** Separate from the 4 gear slots. Only activated if all gear from a gearset is equipped, it provides a unique, special effect as a loyalty bonus to the manufacturer.

Pieces of gear are then split into different tiers determined by their effectiveness, the description provides a rough estimate of how powerful that piece of gear is.

| Tier | Price | Description |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **1** | \$3,000 | Universal, can work regardless of gadget. However, it has a niche use case. |
| **2** | \$5,000 | Universal, can work regardless of gadget. Has a consistent effect during or outside of a fight. |
| **3** | \$10,000 | Usually requires a specific gadget to be effective. Majorly affects action economy, or provides a new use case to your gear. |
| **4** | \$14,000 | Requires a specific gadget or piece of equipment to be effective. Provides a combination of the prior tiers, changes or adds to the mechanics of a piece of equipment, or reduces downsides entirely. |

# **`Glossary`**

## **`Modifiers`**

| Modifier | Catchall term for something that affects the roll. EG: Bonus, Malus, Dice Bonus, Dice Malus |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Bonus** | A \+\[x\] modifier to the roll.EG: \+1 Bonus will modify a 2d6k1 skill roll to 2d6k1+1 Abbreviated to B |
| **Dice Bonus** | A \+\[x\] dice modifier to the rollEG: \+1 Dice Bonus will modify a 2d6k1 skill roll to 3d6k1 Abbreviated to DB |
| **Malus** | A \-\[x\] modifier to the roll EG: \-1 Malus will modify a 2d6k1 skill roll to 2d6k1-1 Abbreviated to M |
| **Dice Malus** | A \-\[x\] dice modifier to the rollEG: \-1 Dice Malus will modify a 2d6k1 skill roll to 1d6Abbreviated to DM |

## **`Terms`**

| Game Master | The star of the show, equivalent to a Dungeon master.Abbreviated to GM. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Player** | A player controlling a character. |
| **Character** | Someone within the world, they are assigned stats just like anyone else, and can either be controlled by the Game Master or a Player. Doesn't necessarily have to be a ‘Human’.GMPC is the term for characters controlled by Game Masters.PC is the term for characters controlled by Players |
| **Margin of Success (MOS)** | A character's degree of success in a test. In action tests, if a character meets or surpasses a threshold, the margin of success is their result minus the threshold. In opposed action tests, it's their result minus their opponent's result. EG: a result of 5 against a threshold of 2 is an MOS of 3 (5 − 2 \= 3).  |
| **Margin of Failure (MOF)** | A character's degree of failure in a test. In action tests, if a character fails to meet a threshold, the margin of failure is the threshold minus their result. In opposed action tests, it's their opponent's result minus their own. EG: a result of 4 against a threshold of 6 is an MOF of 2 (6 − 4 \= 2).  |
| **Consume/Spend** | Use a specific resource, such as E-Dice, ammo, grenades, or gadget charges.EG: Consuming 1 E-dice reduces the total amount of E-dice you have stocked by 1\. |
| **Ignore**  | Negate or don’t account for a condition, mechanic, or affliction during tests, under some conditions. |
| **Restore/Restock** | Restore a specific resource, such as E-Dice, ammo,Grenades, or gadget chargesEG: Restoring all your gadget and ammo charges from an ammo bag will return all of your ammo and gadget charges up to your max capacity. |
| **Attacker** | A character prompting an opposed action test against another. |
| **Defender** | A character responding to an opposed action test against another. |
| **Target** | A character specified as the defender of an opposed action test. Used in the context of afflictions and effects. |
| **Friendly** | A character that is a member, or allied with your party |
| **Hostile/Contact** | A character that is not a part of, or against your party. |
| **Wound Overflow** | Wound Overflow prevents instant death. When a character takes an instance of damage that beats their instant death threshold, and is either from a source with this tag or does an action that causes damage taken to be wound overflow, take a deep wound and subtract the deep wound threshold from the total damage. Repeat this as long as the remaining damage still meets that threshold. Once it doesn’t, repeat this against the flesh wound threshold and take flesh wounds until the remaining damage passes neither threshold. |
| **Temporary Wounds** | Temporary Wounds are wounds that are gained via CQC attacks and actions during Close Combat. These wounds can be removed by using a **\[Recover\]** action, or go away once you exit combat. |
| **Multi Action Penalty (MAP)** | Occurs when a character initiates a test from both their bonus and standard actions in the same turn, whichever action is taken second incurs a \-1 malus to all rolls related to it. This malus applies and is only caused from tests the character chooses to make. It does not apply to tests forced by an external source (e.g. Suppression) or opposed checks made as the defender. |
| **Combat Batch** | A group of characters in the initiative queue from the same faction or party. |

## **`Afflictions`**

| Pinged | Applies \+1 bonus to offensive shooting or throwing actions targeting this character. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Marked** | Applies \+2 bonus to offensive shooting or throwing actions targeting this character. |
| **Suppressed** | When **suppressed**, any character not in cover from the source of suppression is immediately knocked prone. At the start of their turn, they must pass a Spirit test against a threshold of 4 or take a \-1 dice malus to all rolls and lose the ability to move or make offensive actions for the turn. If the character makes an offensive action, they roll armor against the saturation fire threshold for damage. Removed when the character leaves the line of fire from the source of suppression. |
| **Aflame** | When **aflame**, at the end of a character's turn, the character will roll their armor against the burn threshold, which is 3+ the the result of the initial roll that caused the effect, and resolve the damage with **Wound Overflow**. Removed when the character uses **\[Patch Up\]** or someone else does on them. Ignore 2 stage armor systems. |
| **Tased** | When **tased**, at the start of a character's turn, they must make a Body test against a threshold of 4\. Upon failure, if a weapon is in hand, they immediately fire uncontrollably towards the closest character to them, which counts as their full action for the turn. If no weapon is in hand, they lose their full action instead. |
| **Bleeding** | When **bleeding**, at the start of a character’s turn, they take one flesh wound. Removed when the character uses **\[Patch Up\]** or someone else does on them. |
| **Winded** | Applies \-1 malus to the character’s next action. |
| **Concussed** | Applies \-1 malus to all of the character’s actions for the turn. |
| **Exhausted** | Reduces all skills in a category (Alertness, Body, Intelligence, Spirit) to 1 for a specific amount of time  |
| **Diminished**  | Reduces all skills in a category (Alertness, Body, Intelligence, Spirit) to 0 for a specific amount of time  |
| **Crippled** | Reduces the total amount of range bands a character can move by 1\.  |
| **Debilitated** | Removes a character’s ability to move. Applies \-1 to all movement related tests. |
| **Severed** | Disables a character’s ability to use **Datalink** enabled equipment, call reinforcements, or use their radio. |
| **Disabled** | Renders a piece of equipment temporarily inoperable. |
| **Burned** | Renders a piece of equipment permanently inoperable. |
| **Down But Not Out (DBNO)** | When wounds surpass your **System Shock** threshold, or an instance of damage beats a character’s instant death threshold (and they don't use an E-dice) they enter a Down But Not Out state. At the start of each turn they must make a **Body test** with a threshold of 4\. Failing the body test 3 times causes them to become **Incapacitated.**   |
| **Incapacitated** | When incapacitated, a character is hanging onto life by a thread. They are close to death, and cannot make any actions. They are functionally out of the fight. |
